# Shin et al. (2019): rat CA1 and prefrontal cortex learning a W-maze in a day

**The question.** How does awake replay at the reward wells change while a rat
learns a spatial alternation task, and does prefrontal cortex take part? Six
rats learned a W-maze in one day, over eight run sessions between rest sessions
in a sleep box, with CA1 and prefrontal cortex (PFC) recorded throughout on
tetrodes. This dataset has the trials (start and end well, outbound or inbound,
correct or not), position, sorted CA1 and PFC units and the LFP of every
tetrode, so it suits awake replay at reward wells, place-field templates per
trajectory, CA1-PFC coordination and learning curves.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Dataset id | `shin_2019` |
| Animal and region | rat (8 animals, 6 in the paper), dorsal CA1 and prefrontal cortex |
| Apparatus | 80 x 80 cm W-maze with 7 cm wide track sections and a reward well at the end of each arm; rest in a 30 x 30 cm sleep box |
| Archive | DANDI 000978, version 0.240511.0307 (CC-BY-4.0); NWB files of 28 to 47 GB, read by byte range |
| Sessions | 9 files, one per rat (ZT2's day is split across two). The default is `sub-JDS-SingleDay-JS14` (72 units, the smallest file) |

## What was recorded

Shin, Tang & Jadhav (2019), Neuron 104:1110, recorded "the same stable
ensembles in dorsal CA1 ... and PFC" for "all 8 sessions over the course of
learning" in one day. Its STAR Methods describe:

- the day: "eight 15-20 min sessions on a W-maze interleaved with 20-30 min rest
  sessions in a sleep box";
- the maze: "80 x 80 cm with 7-cm-wide track sections", with three reward wells
  at the ends of its arms. Returning to the centre well after either side well
  was rewarded, and so was choosing, from the centre, the side well not visited
  last;
- tracking: "an overhead color CCD camera (30 fps)" and "color LEDs affixed to
  the headstage";
- LFP: "sampled at 1.5 kHz and bandpass filtered between 0.5 Hz and 400 Hz",
  recorded "relative to the local reference tetrode".

Each file holds the sorted units with their tetrode, the position (x, y and the
authors' velocity), the 17 epochs (8 run and 9 rest), the trials, and the LFP
of both channels of every tetrode.

**Which paper.** The dandiset names no paper. The link is inferred: the paper's
six rats (ER1, KL8, JS14, JS15, JS17, JS21; its Table S1 and Figure S1) are
subjects of the dandiset, the task and day match, and those six files hold 1,537
correct trials against the 1,533 of the paper's per-visit figures. JS34 and ZT2
are in the dandiset but not in the paper; they are offered, and no paper number
describes them.

**Why the files are not downloaded.** About half of each file is
`acquisition/ElectricalSeries`, which holds the same samples as the LFP (equal at
every block compared: three random blocks in each of the nine files). `load()`
reads only the units, position, epochs and trials by byte range: 47 MB in 20 s
for JS14. `rec.lfp.fetch(channels, stream=True)` reads the LFP the same way;
since the file stores each sample's 60 to 128 channels together, that reads 14 to
24 GB whatever the channels, so ask for all you need in one call.

| trials: as stored; well 1 is the centre well, `trajectory_type` 0 outbound | inferred | Every outbound trial starts at well 1, whose arm is the middle one. |
| a trial ends as the rat leaves its end well | inferred | On JS14, at a trial's stop the authors' velocity is a median 16 cm/s and rising, and the rat is a median 18 cm from the end of its end well's arm. The stay at a well is the end of the trial before; the infrared beam times the paper used are not in the file. |
| `units`: `brain_region` from the electrodes table's `location` of each unit's tetrode; `tetrode` kept | inferred | The units table's `electrodes` column holds tetrode numbers, not rows of the electrodes table (see Watch out). |
| environment: the W-maze as a track graph (three arms joined by a base), its six nodes fitted to the run epochs' samples, 2 cm bins; the arms' ends as regions `well_1` to `well_3` | inferred | No source gives the nodes. On JS14 the arms are 87 to 99 cm long and their feet 46 cm apart; the samples span 117 x 109 cm against the stated 80 x 80 cm, since the LEDs swing past the arms' ends. |
| LFP: 1,500 Hz, µV, the series' own timestamps; channels `tetrodeN Ref ON` and `tetrodeN Ref OFF` | stated | The series' unit "microvolts", conversion 1. |

The 30 Hz frame rate is kept as **inferred**, not stated: the frame times
alternate between steps of about 29 and 39 ms (median 30.7 ms, 32.6 Hz), which
would refuse every session against a stated 30 Hz. The position span must lie
between 64 and 150.2 cm.

## Watch out

- **pynwb reads the units' brain areas wrong.** The units table's `electrodes`
  column holds each unit's tetrode number, not a row of the electrodes table.
  Read as tetrode numbers, each of the paper's six rats has at least as many CA1
  and PFC units as its continuously tracked CA1 place cells and PFC cells
  (Figure S1F-G); read as rows, as pynwb and pynapple do, JS14 would have 31 CA1
  units for its 37 place cells, KL8 22 PFC units for 25 cells and JS17 42 for 43.
  On JS14, 35 of the 72 units land in the wrong area that way.
  `rec.units["brain_region"]` reads them as tetrodes.
- **Referenced and unreferenced LFP.** Each tetrode has two channels: `Ref ON`,
  referenced to its region's reference tetrode, and `Ref OFF`, not referenced.
  The reference tetrodes (in corpus callosum for CA1 and overlying cortex for
  PFC, by the paper) are labelled with their region; their two channels are
  equal. On JS14 the CA1 tetrodes are referenced to tetrode 14 and the PFC ones
  to tetrode 1. Detect ripples on CA1 `Ref ON` channels, as the paper's
  ripple-band LFPs were referenced.
- **The sleep box shares the camera frame.** Its positions overlap the maze's
  (75% of JS14's rest samples fall in a bin of `rec.env`). Use
  `params["analysis_epochs"]`, the run epochs.
- **No interneuron labels, no place-cell list, no ripples.** The paper excluded
  putative interneurons by spike width and rate and kept cells stable over all
  eight sessions; the files hold every sorted unit. The files have no ripple
  table and name no ripple channel.
- **JS34 and ZT2 are not in the paper**, and ZT2's day is split across two
  files (9 and 8 epochs) whose units were sorted separately.
- **Held positions.** 39% of JS14's samples repeat the one before, in runs of 11
  or more (the longest 92 s), most in the sleep box.

## Analysis choices

`rec.params`: `bin_size` 2 cm (the paper's linearized bins); `min_speed` 5 cm/s
and `immobility_speed` 4 cm/s (the paper's movement and immobility thresholds);
`speed_smooth_window` 0.5 s (see the speed conversion); `analysis_epochs` the run
epochs; `train_epochs` and `test_epochs`, the trials alternating within each
start-end path; `dt` 0.5 s and `replay_dt` 0.01 s, the paper's decoding bins
during running and during SWRs.

## Worked result

**Status: not possible yet; attempted on the paper's six rats, five of six
conditions met.** Over 1,488 well visits we find 9.46 s of immobility, 6.68
SWRs, 2.98 candidate events and 1.02 replay events per visit, against the
paper's 10.3 s, 7.3, 2.3 and 0.9, each within the mark fixed before the run;
62.2% of visits hold two or more candidates (paper 53.4%, within the mark), but
75.5% hold two or more SWRs (paper 85.7%), 10.2 points apart against a mark of
10. The visits, the interneuron labels and the ripple tetrodes are our
readings of what the files lack, listed below.

**The paper's numbers.** For each correct trial, the paper counts SWRs,
candidate events and replay events "detected in immobility periods per choice
(i.e., per transition)": "immobility time: 10.3 ± 5.7 s; SWRs: 7.3 ± 5.5 events;
candidate events: 2.3 ± 2.4 events; replay events: 0.9 ± 1.2 events; data are
presented in mean ± SD" (Figure 2G-I), and "immobility periods with ≥2 events,
85.7% or 1,313/1,533 trials for SWRs; 53.4% or 818/1,533 trials for replay
candidate events" (Results).

**How we count them.** Each step follows the paper's STAR Methods where it
states one; where the file lacks what the paper used, the choice is ours and
is named.

- *The visit.* A trial's visit is the stay at its start well, before it runs
  ("the immobility period at the well during a given behavioral sequence";
  Table S2 pairs each trajectory with the well it starts from). The paper's
  entry is "the first time point when the speed fell below 4 cm/s before the
  arrival trigger" and its exit "the first time point when the speed rose above
  4 cm/s after the departure trigger", from an infrared beam at the well. The
  beam times are not in the file, so arrival and departure are the first and
  last sample of the rat's last stay within 15 cm of the well (the paper's
  reward-well radius), entry the first sample from arrival at or below 4 cm/s,
  and exit the start of the last stretch above 4 cm/s that reaches departure.
  Speed is `compute_speed(..., smooth_window=0.5)`, which matches the file's
  velocity column.
- *SWRs.* `ripple_detection.literature_methods.shin_2019` (the Karlsson rule:
  ripple band 150-250 Hz, Hilbert envelope, 3 SD on at least one tetrode,
  bounds at the mean, speed at most 4 cm/s), run per run session on the `Ref
  ON` channel of every CA1 tetrode holding a sorted unit (no source names the
  ripple tetrodes). An SWR belongs to a visit when it starts inside it.
- *Candidates.* The same detector's `stage="decoding_candidates"`: SWRs of at
  least 50 ms in which at least 5 place cells fire. Place cells are CA1 units
  with at least 100 spikes in every run session ("Units included in analyses
  fired at least 100 spikes in each session") whose peak in that session's
  trajectory maps is at least 3 Hz. The paper also excluded putative
  interneurons by spike width and rate, which the file cannot give.
- *Templates.* For each run session and each of the four trajectories
  (centre to well 2, well 2 to centre, centre to well 3, well 3 to centre), the
  linearized map of every place cell from that trajectory's trials, at more
  than 5 cm/s, in 2 cm bins smoothed with a 4 cm SD Gaussian, bins with more
  than 20 ms of occupancy, cut 15 cm from each well ("we excluded the spatial
  positions within 15 cm of reward wells from the place-field templates").
- *Replay.* `SHIN_2019` unchanged, on `decode_jointly` over the four
  trajectories in 10 ms bins: 10,000 posterior samples per bin, R² of the line
  fit against 1,500 shuffles of the bins' order, the trajectory with the
  smallest p, replay at p < 0.05. Each event's shuffles are seeded
  (`[2019, session, event]`).

The pass mark was fixed before any SWR was detected, after the visits were
counted on JS14: each of the four per-visit means within half the paper's SD
of its mean, and each share of visits with two or more events within 10
percentage points.

**What we measured about the SWR gap.** The pooled SWR count is within the
mark, but the rats differ: JS14 and KL8 have fewer than 4 SWRs per visit, JS17
more than 10, and fewer visits than the paper's hold two or more. We name no
single cause:

- The detector requires the envelope to stay above 3 SD for at least 15 ms, a
  minimum `ripple_detection` takes from Karlsson & Frank (2009); the paper
  states none ("contiguous periods when the envelope stayed above 3 SD of the
  mean on at least one tetrode"). Without it, JS14 has 7.14 SWRs per visit,
  against the paper's 7.3, but the other rats have 16 to 29, and the six 19.2.
  So the minimum explains JS14's gap and not the pooled share.
- The ripple tetrodes are not named. On all 14 non-reference CA1 tetrodes of
  JS14, rather than the 6 holding units, its run sessions hold 13% more SWRs
  (measured once while building this page, not pinned by a test).
- Our visits are shorter (9.46 s against 10.3 s), shortest for ER1 and KL8,
  since the beam times are replaced by a 15 cm radius; a short visit holds
  fewer SWRs.

The detector is left as it is: these are measurements, not fixes.

**The lab's code.** The Jadhav lab's replay script
(JadhavLab/PrefrontalRipples, `ProcessData/jds_replay_decoding_CA1_M.m` at
72f0f3e, written for Shin & Jadhav 2024; no code of the 2019 paper is public)
draws 10,000 samples in all, split among the time bins by spike count,
averages R² over 500 draws, draws each shuffle once, and counts p without +1.
Run on the same joint decodes, it calls 162 of JS14's 735 candidates replay
(0.65 per visit) against `SHIN_2019`'s 180: 36 only here, 18 only there. Over
the six rats' 4,430 candidates it calls 1,500 against 1,524 (1.01 and 1.02 per
visit): 192 only here and 168 only there. The differences are its sampling and
its p-value without +1, taken together. `SHIN_2019` keeps the paper's text.

**Checks on the data.** The units' areas are read from tetrode numbers (see
Watch out): CA1 units fire a median 1.40 to 2.56 times their run-session rate
inside SWRs over the six rats, PFC units 0.74 to 0.98 times. On JS14 no PFC
unit doubles its rate; on ER1 one does (4.6 times).

The tests are `test_per_visit_counts_on_js14`,
`test_lab_code_changes_verdicts_both_ways_on_js14`,
`test_ca1_units_fire_in_swrs_and_pfc_units_do_not` and
`test_per_visit_counts_of_the_six_rats` in `tests/datasets/test_shin_2019.py`
(the first three read 5.5 GB by byte range, about 30 minutes; the last about
40 GB).
