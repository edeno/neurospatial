# Diba & Buzsáki (2007): forward and reverse sequences at the ends of a linear track (rat)

**The question.** When a rat stops at the end of a linear track, do its place
cells fire in the order of the run it has just made, or of the run it is about
to make? Diba & Buzsáki (2007) recorded CA1 and CA3 with silicon probes while
rats ran back and forth for water, and found both: at the start end, before a
run, place-cell sequences recurred in forward order (preplay); at the far end,
after it, in reverse (replay). This page loads Diba's sessions from CRCNS
hc-3 and runs the paper's event detection and rank-order test on the one
session whose positions are public.

## What was recorded

The Supplementary Methods describe three rats trained to run back and forth
on linear tracks of 79, 125 and 170 cm (6.2 cm wide) with 21 x 21 cm platforms
at the ends, implanted with 32- or 64-site silicon probes (4 or 8 shanks of 8
sites) in left dorsal CA1 and CA3, and recorded with a Neuralynx DigitaLynx at
32,552 Hz; the LFP is low-passed to 1,252 Hz and "the position was tracked
with an LED". Each session in hc-3 holds:

- the sorted spikes of every shank (`.res.N`, `.clu.N`); hc-3 sorted all the
  sessions of one day together, so a unit keeps its id `"<shank>.<cluster>"`
  across a day's sessions;
- the LFP of every site (`.eeg`), fetched only on request: `rec.lfp`
  describes each channel by its shank and region;
- hc-3's cell table gives each unit's type (`rec.units["label"]`: pyramidal,
  interneuron or unidentified) and its electrode table each shank's region
  (`rec.units["brain_region"]`).

**hc-3 holds no position for any of these 27 sessions.** Every archive was
read member by member: none has a `.whl` file. Their `rec.times` is the LFP's
sample clock (1,252 Hz), so the recording's span is known, and there is no
environment or speed.

**One session has positions from elsewhere.** Maboudi et al. (2018), whose
authors include Diba, released `2006-6-7_16-40-19` as a "sample recording
epoch" (`data/fig1.nel` in their code repository), with the rat's 2-D
position at 60 Hz. Its spikes, Diba's own sorting of the same recording, lie
on hc-3's clock: 95.3% of its 258,427 spike times fall within 1 µs of an hc-3
detected spike of this session, none when shifted 0.1 s either way. In that
session the track was shortened partway: `rec.epochs["long_track"]` (0 to
1,180 s, 254 cm along the track with the platforms) and
`rec.epochs["short_track"]` (1,250 to 2,580 s, 164 cm, which matches the
paper's 125 cm track and two 21 cm platforms; the long one matches none of
its tracks).

## Which sessions are the paper's

No source lists the paper's 69 sessions (9, 40 and 20 per rat). Maboudi et
al. (2018) analysed 18 linear-track sessions of Diba's rats "previously used
by [Diba and Buzsáki, 2007]" and their `Figure2.ipynb` prints the list; 6 of
them are in hc-3 (`diba_2007.PAPER_SESSIONS`). Each registry row says whether
its session is one of the six (`paper_session`, `paper_link`), and each
recording's first watch-out says it again. The other six of Maboudi's list
are not in hc-3 (`ABSENT_PAPER_SESSIONS`); hc-3's original README says
`gor01-6-8` was removed.

## Where the data come from, and their terms

hc-3 is downloaded from CRCNS with your own account and never redistributed.
A `.tar.gz` cannot be read by byte range, so loading a session streams its
archive once and keeps only the spike, cluster and parameter files (and the
metadata tables); fetching the LFP streams it again up to the `.eeg` file.
The default session's positions are downloaded from eLife's archived copy of
Maboudi et al.'s repository (`github.com/elifesciences-publications/UncoveringTemporalStructureHippocampus`,
commit f1b021d, 16.3 MB). **No data license is stated for that file**: the
paper says these data "are available from Kamran Diba on request" and that
"sample recording epochs ... are available" in the repository, whose MIT
license covers its code. The file is a `dill` pickle of `nelpy` objects; it
is read without running any of it (`nelpy` classes become inert containers,
and the functions inside its HMM and tuning-curve objects inert stand-ins).

## Units and conversions

| What the loader did | Basis | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| spike times: `.res` samples / 32,552 Hz | stated | each session's `.xml`; the Supplementary Methods' 32,552 Hz |
| units: clusters from 2 up; region from hc-3's electrode table; label from its cell table | stated | hc-3's data description: clusters 0 (noise) and 1 (unsortable) "should not be used" |
| LFP: int16 x 0.305 µV, sample k at k / 1,252 s | stated | the `.xml`'s 20 V range, 16 bits and gain of 1,000 |
| times, without positions: the LFP's sample clock | stated | the `.xml`'s LFP rate and the `.eeg` file's size |
| positions (default session): Maboudi et al.'s `pos2d`, x and y in cm | inferred | the file states no unit; their figures label it cm, and the short segment spans 164 cm against the paper's 125 cm track plus platforms (167 cm) |
| positions' clock | inferred | the file's spikes are hc-3's spikes to 1 µs (95.3%) |
| `long_track`, `short_track` | stated | the file's two support intervals, which nelpy's hc-3 table names "long" and "short" |
| laps per direction: leaving one end platform, entering the other | inferred | the Supplementary Methods' definition of a trajectory; the platforms placed 21 cm from the samples' ends along the track |
| environment: a straight track along the long segment's samples, 2 cm bins | inferred | no source states the long track's length or placement |

## Watch out

- **Units are hc-3's sorting, not the paper's.** Ackermann's note in nelpy's
  hc-3 reader: "Kamran just has a different (much better) unit sorting than
  what's available on CRCNS." On the default session, Diba's sorting has 103
  units (interneurons left out) against hc-3's 87 clusters, and only 62.3% of
  its spikes are hc-3 sorted spikes. This matters for the worked result below.
- **No positions in hc-3.** 26 sessions load with spikes, units and LFP only.
- **The default session's positions are processed**, not raw tracking: no
  sample is missing and steps are smooth to a hundredth of a cm. How lost LED
  frames were filled in is not documented.
- **The track changes length mid-session.** Analyse the two segments apart;
  `rec.env` is the long track, and 5% of samples lie outside it by
  construction (its width is a placement tolerance set from the samples).
- **The positions end at 2,579.98 s**; the last spike is at 2,587.80 s.

## Worked result

**Status: not possible yet.**

The finding: "the majority (841 out of 887) of positively correlated
significant events occurred at the start end of the track before running
(preplay), whereas the negatively correlated significant events (395 out of
464) occurred at the other end following the run (replay)": 95% and 85%
(Fig. 1c).

The test, fixed in writing before the run (`WORKED_RESULT["attempted"]`): on
each track segment of the default session and each running direction, place
cells are pyramidal units whose rate map over that direction's laps peaks at
5 Hz or more; candidate events are `ripple_detection`'s `diba_2007` (60 ms of
silence of the place cells, then at least 5 or 30% of them firing in the next
300 ms, speed at most 10 cm/s at both ends), kept inside a platform; each is
judged with `DIBA_BUZSAKI_2007` (first spikes, 500 cell-identity shuffles,
more correlated than 95% of them either way) against its direction's
template, and an event found for both directions goes to the template the
preset's `template_choice` picks. Reproduced when, pooled over the segments,
more than half the significant forward events are before the run and more
than half the reverse ones after it, each with a one-sided binomial p < 0.05.

| | Events | Forward | Before the run | Reverse | After the run |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| long track | 224 | 8 | 4 (50%) | 12 | 11 (92%) |
| short track | 490 | 21 | 14 (67%) | 33 | 29 (88%) |
| pooled | 714 | 29 | **18 (62.1%, p = 0.13)** | 45 | **40 (88.9%, p < 1e-7)** |
| paper | 2,450 | 887 | 841 (95%) | 464 | 395 (85%) |

The reverse half reproduces; the forward half does not, so the test is not
met. Two differences, measured (`diba_2007.REPLAY_CHECK`):

- **With hc-3's units the significant events are no more than chance**: 4.1%
  forward and 6.3% reverse against the 5% each the test passes by chance
  (binomial p = 0.89 and 0.069), where the paper found 36% and 19%. Run on
  Diba's own sorting of the same spikes (Maboudi et al.'s file), the same
  pipeline finds 22.6% significant (14.2% forward, 8.4% reverse, each above
  chance, p < 0.001), a forward to reverse ratio of 1.7 (the paper's 1.9) and
  82.5% of reverse events after the run: the sorting accounts for the excess
  of significant events.
- **It does not account for the forward share**: Diba's sorting gives 57.4%
  of forward events before the run (p = 0.051), still far from 95%.
  Candidate events are also about five times as frequent as the paper's 0.1
  per second of immobility (0.47 and 0.54 per second on the two segments).
  One session cannot say whether this is the session or the pipeline: the
  paper pools 69.
